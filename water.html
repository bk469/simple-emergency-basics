The essentials

Key Points

  • Plan for 1 gallon of water per person, per day — more in hot weather, for illness, children, older adults, or pets.
  • Clear water is not necessarily safe water. Treat any water from an outdoor source before drinking it.
  • Boiling kills most disease-causing germs, but it does not remove chemicals, fuel, or pesticides.
  • Unscented household bleach is a reliable backup disinfectant — never guess the amount, always check the label.
  • If you suspect chemical, fuel, or industrial contamination, do not boil and drink it — find another source.
  • Keep untreated and treated water in two separate, clearly marked containers to avoid recontamination.

Two dependable methods

Step-by-Step Instructions

Boil water (preferred method)

Boiling is the simplest dependable method for killing most disease-causing germs.

  1. Step 1

    If the water is cloudy, let sediment settle or pass the water through a clean cloth or coffee filter.

  2. Step 2

    Bring the water to a rolling boil.

  3. Step 3

    Boil for 1 minute. At elevations above approximately 6,500 feet, boil for 3 minutes.

  4. Step 4

    Allow the water to cool naturally.

  5. Step 5

    Put it into a clean, covered container.

If you cannot boil: bleach disinfection (backup method)

Ordinary unscented household chlorine bleach can be an emergency backup. Check the label first.

For clear water and bleach containing 5%–9% sodium hypochlorite:

1 gallon+8 drops
  1. Stir well.
  2. Wait at least 30 minutes.
  3. Keep the treated water covered.

For cloudy, murky, colored, or very cold water, CDC guidance calls for double the amount.

Never use
  • Scented bleach.
  • Color-safe bleach.
  • Bleach mixed with cleaners.
  • Products when you do not know what they contain.

Never guess. Check the label and current public-health instructions.

When water cannot be made safe

Do not assume every contaminated water source can be repaired by boiling or filtering. Water suspected of containing gasoline, fuel oil, pesticides, toxic chemicals, or industrial contamination should not simply be boiled and consumed. Find another water source.

What you need and where to look

Supplies & Options

Where can you find water?

Possible sources include:

  • Stored drinking water.
  • Bottled water.
  • A safe public water supply.
  • A safe well.
  • Household plumbing.
  • An appropriate household water heater.
  • Rainwater.
  • Springs.
  • Streams and rivers.
  • Lakes and ponds.

Outdoor water should normally be treated before drinking. Clear water is not necessarily safe water.

What about water filters?

Filters can be extremely useful. But remember:

A filter is not automatically a purifier.

Different filters remove different things. Some remove parasites. Some remove bacteria. Many do not reliably remove viruses.

Before depending on a filter, know:

  • What it removes and what it does not remove.
  • How often it must be cleaned or replaced.
  • Whether the water also needs to be disinfected.

Follow the manufacturer’s instructions.

Free and inexpensive options

Low-Cost Solutions

  • Boiling costs nothing beyond fuel you may already have — no special equipment required.
  • Unscented household bleach is inexpensive and treats large amounts of water; a small bottle can disinfect many gallons.
  • A clean cloth or coffee filter clarifies cloudy water before disinfecting — no purchase needed.
  • Reuse food-grade containers you already own instead of buying specialty water storage.
  • Rainwater collected in clean containers can supplement your supply at no cost — always treat it before drinking.

The two-container rule

This one free habit can prevent many problems. Keep two containers clearly marked:

Container 1Untreated water
Container 2Safe drinking water

Never put purified water back into a dirty container. Do not dip dirty hands, cups, or utensils into stored drinking water. A covered container with a pouring spout is best.

Avoid these

Common mistakes

  • Assume clear water is safe.
  • Drink directly from a stream because it looks clean.
  • Put clean water into a dirty container.
  • Guess at bleach amounts.
  • Assume every camping filter removes every danger.
  • Waste drinking water when untreated water could serve another purpose.
  • Assume boiling removes chemicals.

Try this now

Water practice

Take one gallon of water and pretend the normal water supply has stopped.

  1. Find a way to filter visible sediment.
  2. Boil the water.
  3. Cool it.
  4. Put it into a clean container.
  5. Label it Safe Water.

Could I still do this if the electricity and running water were both off?

If not, you have discovered what you need to prepare.

Before an emergency

Quick checklist

  • I know how much drinking water I have.
  • I know where additional water could come from.
  • I can boil water without grid electricity.
  • I have clean water containers.
  • I know how my water filter works.
  • I have a backup purification method.
  • I can keep untreated water separate from safe water.

Remember

Water does not have to look dirty to be dangerous.

Boiling is usually the simplest way to kill germs.

Chemical contamination requires another source.
