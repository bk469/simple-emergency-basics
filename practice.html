The essentials

Key Points

  • Choose one skill and one clear goal at a time. Do not test too many systems before you have mastered the basics.
  • Follow the same simple cycle every time: Choose → Do → Observe → Improve.
  • Never turn off smoke or carbon monoxide alarms, essential medical equipment, medicine refrigeration, or needed utilities to make a drill feel real. End the drill if conditions become unsafe.
  • Practice with more than one person, so one household member does not hold all the knowledge and skill.
  • Write down what worked, what failed and what was missing while it is still fresh. Give every fix an owner and a date.
  • Practice again on a regular schedule, not just once. Skills fade when you do not use them.

What to do

Step-by-Step Instructions

One skill, one objective, one improvement

You do not need a dramatic survival exercise. Begin with a short task you can finish safely. Make it harder only after the basics work.

Choose→Do→Observe→Improve
  • Choose: Name exactly what you want to prove.
  • Do: Use the real supplies, people and written instructions.
  • Observe: Note the time, fuel, water, missing items, confusion and safety concerns.
  • Improve: Give every fix a person and a date.

Practice the essential systems

Water

Make safe water

Do: Find containers, clear cloudy water if needed, use your planned treatment method, cool it safely, store it cleanly, and label it. Record: treatment time, fuel used, how much you made, and whether you could do it without electricity.

Food

Cook one outage meal

Do: Make a meal from familiar stored food without the electric stove, the refrigerator, or a trip to the store. Record: water, fuel, time to make it, portions, how satisfying it was, and missing tools.

Sprouting

Grow a small batch

Do: Sprout lentils or mung beans with clean equipment and safe water. Record: amount of seed, rinsing schedule, days to harvest, water used, how much you got, smell, texture, and whether the batch stayed clean.

Sanitation

Set up, but do not use

Do: Line the toilet or bucket with a strong bag, set out absorbent material and paper, choose a protected place for waste, and make a handwashing station. Record: setup time and missing hygiene supplies.

Fire and heat

Operate the approved system

Do: Outdoors, or in an approved, properly vented setup, practice starting, controlling and shutting down the system, and boiling a large pot of water. Check: fuel, safe distances, fresh air, alarms and extinguisher. Never make up your own way of burning fuel indoors.

Tools

Complete a small task

Do: Use a hand saw, hammer, shovel, adjustable wrench, or another tool you would expect to need, for an ordinary job. Record: its condition, how sharp it is, protective gear, effort, upkeep needed, and where it is stored.

First aid

Open and use the kit

Do: Find gloves, gauze, bandages, tape, cold packs and medicines. Practice a simple bandage on a volunteer who is not hurt. Check: dates, medicine lists, training, and the nearest AED. Use training equipment for advanced skills.

Communication

Work without internet

Do: Turn off Wi-Fi on one device, find the radio, tune a station you know, use a printed contact list, and review the meeting places and the out-of-area contact. Record: battery condition and any information missing from the paper copies.

Try a short power-outage period

For a few planned hours, go without things you do not need, such as main lights, the electric stove, television and home internet. Keep essential systems on, and tell everyone when the exercise starts and ends.

See

Use flashlights, headlamps or lanterns you have set out, and move safely.

Eat

Make one simple stored meal with your approved backup method.

Communicate

Use printed contacts, the radio and the household meeting plan.

Conserve

Track how much battery, fuel and water you use instead of guessing.

Keep it safe: Do not unplug refrigerators that hold essential medicines or food that cannot safely warm up. Do not turn off medical devices, alarms, sump pumps, heating needed to stay safe, or any system that could cause damage if it stopped.

Practice with other people

One person should not be the only one who knows how to treat water, run approved equipment, find supplies, shut off water, or call for help. Let each person do a task that fits their age and ability while another person watches.

Simple practice list

  • Make water safe the right way
  • Boil water without electricity
  • Cook one emergency meal
  • Sprout a small batch of seeds
  • Set up an emergency toilet
  • Create a handwashing station
  • Run an approved heat or cooking system
  • Use basic hand tools safely
  • Find and use the first-aid kit
  • Find emergency lights in the dark
  • Charge an essential device
  • Tune the emergency radio
  • Find the water, electrical and fuel shutoffs
  • Practice the meeting and contact plan

Free and inexpensive options

Low-Cost Solutions

  • Practice drills use supplies you already own. You do not need to buy anything new to run one.
  • A plain notebook is enough for the review table. You do not need an app or software.
  • Combine practice with a task you would do anyway, such as cooking a meal you already planned, using your backup method.
  • Use a free calendar app or a wall calendar to keep drills on a repeating schedule at no cost.
  • Invite neighbors or family to share a practice session and split the planning.

Avoid these

Common mistakes

  • Buy equipment and never use it or take care of it.
  • Store food you have never made with the backup method.
  • Assume every household member knows the plan.
  • Make the drill dangerous to make it feel real.
  • Test too many systems at once before you have mastered the basics.
  • Depend only on instructions stored online.
  • Ignore access, medical, child, elder or pet needs.
  • See a failure but never give anyone the fix or check that it was done.
  • Make practice so complicated that no one repeats it.

Try this now

The one-hour challenge

Pretend you have lost electricity and running water, without actually turning off anything essential or unsafe.

  1. Find working emergency lights.
  2. Find and measure the drinking water you have.
  3. Find your approved backup cooking method and fuel.
  4. Set up the emergency toilet and handwashing station without using them.
  5. Open the first-aid kit and find the supplies for stopping bleeding.
  6. Find the radio, power it, and tune it.
  7. Find the most important hand tools and utility shutoffs.
  8. Write down every item that is missing, expired, unclear or hard to reach.

Pick the most important gap, fix it, and repeat that part of the drill.

Before an emergency

Quick checklist

  • I have really treated water with my planned method.
  • I have cooked a meal with my approved backup system.
  • I have sprouted seeds successfully.
  • I can set up emergency sanitation and handwashing.
  • I have used and cared for my basic hand tools.
  • I know what is in the first-aid kit and where it is.
  • I have tested flashlights, radios, batteries and ways to charge.
  • I can find important shutoffs without searching online.
  • Other people know the plan and can do the essential tasks.
  • We write down, assign, fix and retest problems we find in practice.

Remember

What you read can be forgotten.

What you practice becomes familiar.

What becomes familiar is easier to do under stress.
