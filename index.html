Fast emergency help

What is happening right now?

Choose the problem that needs attention. Start with these immediate actions, then open the full guide if you need more detail.

No safe drinking water

Do this first

  1. Use stored or bottled drinking water first.
  2. If water may contain germs, boil it or use an approved disinfection method.
  3. If fuel, chemicals, or toxins may be present, find another water source.

Do not: Assume clear water is safe to drink.

Open the water guide →
No heat or the home is too cold

Do this first

  1. Put on dry layers, hats, gloves, and blankets before trying to heat the whole building.
  2. Move everyone into one smaller, safe room or area.
  3. Use only heating equipment approved for that location, with working smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms.

Never: Run a generator, grill, or other outdoor combustion equipment indoors.

Open the fire and heat guide →
Food is limited

Do this first

  1. Use safe food that will spoil first before opening long-term supplies.
  2. Protect food from contaminated water, chemicals, pests, and moisture.
  3. Cook simply, conserve fuel and water, and distribute food fairly.

Do not: Eat food that smells wrong, looks unsafe, or may have been contaminated.

Open the food guide →
The toilet will not work

Do this first

  1. Determine whether only the water supply failed or the sewer, septic, or drain system failed.
  2. If the waste system failed, stop flushing and set up a lined emergency toilet.
  3. Use absorbent material, seal waste, and keep it away from people, food, and water.

Important: Wash hands after toilet use or handling waste.

Open the sanitation guide →
Someone is hurt or seriously ill

Do this first

  1. Call 911 or local emergency services for any immediately life-threatening condition.
  2. For severe bleeding, apply firm, steady pressure with clean gauze or cloth.
  3. If the person is unresponsive and not breathing normally, begin CPR and use an AED according to your training.

Do not: Delay emergency care to continue reading.

Open the first aid guide →
The power is out

Do this first

  1. Use a flashlight or headlamp before moving through dark rooms.
  2. Check medical devices, immediate hazards, official alerts, and indoor temperature.
  3. Keep refrigerator doors closed and begin conserving phone and battery power.

Never: Operate a generator inside a house, garage, basement, or near doors and windows.

Open the power and communication guide →
I need the essential supplies

Start here

  1. Secure drinking water, essential prescriptions, and necessary medical equipment.
  2. Gather shelf-stable food, flashlights, batteries, charging power, and a radio.
  3. Add first-aid, sanitation, warmth, cooking, and basic repair supplies.

Remember: Keep supplies together, labeled, and familiar to everyone who may need them.

Why this guide exists

There is no perfect plan for every emergency.

For many years, I have studied and practiced different ways of preparing for emergencies: water, fire, food storage, sprouting, foraging, sanitation, shelter, tools, communication, and other basic survival skills.

We cannot predict every situation or buy everything we might someday need. What we can do is learn the basic skills that are useful in almost any crisis.

01

Water

Water comes first.

Know where water can be found, how to collect and purify it, how much people need, and how to store clean water safely.

  • Find and collect available water.
  • Purify water before drinking it.
  • Store clean water in safe containers.

Never assume clear water is safe to drink.

Find it. Purify it. Protect it.

Open the complete water guide →
02

Fire and Heat

Fire can provide warmth, hot water, cooking, and water purification. If wood is available, know how to harvest and use it safely.

  • Saws, axes, and splitting tools.
  • Safe stoves or fireplaces.
  • Fire extinguishing equipment.

Fire can keep us alive, but fire must always be respected.

Open the complete fire and heat guide →
03

Shelter

Protection from cold, heat, rain, and wind can become essential very quickly. Know which parts of a building are safest and how to improve them.

Basements and interior rooms may remain warmer, but always check for:

  • Flooding, mold, and poor ventilation.
  • Structural problems and fire hazards.
  • Carbon monoxide.

It may be easier to keep one small area warm than an entire building.

07

Sanitation

Sanitation is easy to overlook until toilets and running water stop working. Then it can become one of the biggest problems.

Have a plan for:

  • Human waste, garbage, and cleaning.
  • Handwashing, dishwashing, and food preparation.
  • Keeping waste away from water.

A building with food and heat can still become unhealthy if sanitation fails.

Open the complete sanitation guide →
08

First Aid and Medicine

Keep basic first-aid supplies available and know how to respond to cuts, burns, bleeding, sprains, fever, dehydration, and minor wounds.

  • Keep essential personal medications available whenever possible.
  • Use pain and fever medicines according to labels and appropriate medical guidance.
  • Learn basic first aid before an emergency.

Antibiotics and prescription medicines require appropriate medical knowledge and should not be guessed at or used casually.

Open the complete first aid and medicine guide →
09

Tools

Natural resources are only useful if we can use them. Learn how to use important tools safely before an emergency.

  • Axes and hand saws.
  • Hammers and pliers.
  • Screwdrivers and shovels.
  • Buckets and rope.
  • Tarps and knives.
  • Sharpening and repair supplies.
Open the complete tools guide →
10

Light, Power, and Communication

If electricity fails, have alternatives. Do not depend entirely on cell phones or the internet.

  • Flashlights, headlamps, and batteries.
  • Rechargeable battery banks and solar chargers.
  • Radios, written contacts, and printed instructions.

People need a simple way to share reliable information when normal communication systems are unavailable.

Open the complete light, power, and communication guide →
11

People

People may be the most valuable resource of all.

One person does not need to know everything. A group becomes much stronger when knowledge is shared and responsibilities are divided.

Know who can provide first aid, repair plumbing or electrical systems, cut wood, cook, purify water, repair equipment, grow food, and organize people.

12

Practice

Reading about a survival skill is not the same as doing it. What you have actually done is easier to remember when you are under stress.

  • Purify water and start a safe fire.
  • Cook stored food and sprout seeds.
  • Use hand tools and first-aid supplies.
  • Create an emergency toilet.
  • Practice without electricity or the internet.

When an emergency happens

Solve the most important problem first.

Do not waste precious energy trying to make everything normal. Keep the priorities simple, then solve the next problem.

  1. 01Protect life.
  2. 02Get away from immediate danger.
  3. 03Find safe shelter.
  4. 04Secure drinking water.
  5. 05Establish sanitation.
  6. 06Provide warmth and fire safely.
  7. 07Organize food.
  8. 08Care for injuries and medical needs.
  9. 09Establish communication.
  10. 10Work together.

The basic principle

The goal is not to be prepared for everything.

The goal is to know enough to adapt to almost anything.

Learn the basics.

Keep useful supplies.

Practice what you learn.

Know what resources surround you.

Share knowledge with others.

Work cooperatively.
