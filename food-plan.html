30-day emergency food bridge

30-Day One-Meal-a-Day Food Plan

One person. Thirty days. Nothing assumed to be in stock.

This shelf-stable plan targets roughly 2,000 calories per day, but actual nutrition varies by product and preparation. The same daily ration may be divided into two or more meals without changing the shopping list.

Use this as a short-term bridge, not a permanent diet.

Download Plan PDF

Before relying on this plan

Check the label, the person, and the cooking method

  1. 1

    Verify the B12 label.

    Fortification varies. Use enough nutritional yeast to provide at least 2.4 micrograms of vitamin B12 per day.

  2. 2

    Adapt it to the person.

    One very large meal is not appropriate for everyone. Divide the ration or seek individualized guidance when needed.

  3. 3

    Cook dried beans fully.

    Kidney beans need special care. Do not rely on a slow cooker alone to make raw dried kidney beans safe.

Purpose

A basic 30-day emergency bridge

This plan is for one person for 30 days, assuming nothing is already in stock. It combines grains and legumes for complementary amino acids, plus cooking oil, iodized salt, fortified nutritional yeast, vitamin C powder, moringa powder, seeds, and sprouts.

Important limitation

This menu has not been laboratory-verified and does not guarantee complete nutrition. Vitamin A, calcium, iron, essential fats, and other needs may be inadequate. It is not a permanent diet.

Children, pregnant people, older or frail adults, and people with diabetes, kidney or digestive conditions, eating-disorder histories, allergies, medication needs, or other medical concerns may need a different plan.

Choose rice, rolled oats, or wheat berries for the grain and lentils or fully cooked dried beans for the legume. Add iodized salt and the nutrient boosters listed below.

Add each day

  • B12-fortified nutritional yeast: use the label amount that supplies at least 2.4 mcg B12
  • About 200 mg vitamin C powder, mixed into drinking water
  • 2 teaspoons moringa powder
  • 1 tablespoon sesame or sunflower seeds
  • A handful of sprouts, only when they can be produced and handled safely

Storage

Shelf life depends on packaging

Longer-lived staples

Keep cool, dry, dark, and sealed

White rice, wheat berries, dried beans, and dried lentils can store for years when protected from moisture, insects, rodents, and oxygen. Properly packaged dry beans may remain usable for 10 years or more, but quality and cooking performance decline with age.

Rotate regularly

Potency and germination change

Follow best-by dates for nutritional yeast, moringa, seeds, sprouting seed, and vitamin C powder. Rotate them and test sprouting seed while it still germinates reliably.

Rotate soonest

Oil can become rancid

Buy the freshest oil available, follow its date and storage instructions, and replace it regularly. Once opened, protect it from heat, air, and light.

Ordinary store packaging is not the same as long-term storage packaging. Label every item with the purchase or packing date.

Safe daily preparation

How to prepare each meal

  1. Sort, rinse, and soak as needed.

    Sort out stones and rinse ingredients. Lentils, oats, and rice do not need soaking. Wheat berries may be soaked to save fuel. For beans, follow package directions. A USDA quick soak boils beans for 2 to 3 minutes, then covers them for at least 1 hour before draining.

  2. Cook dried beans safely.

    Cook beans until soft all the way through. Red kidney beans need particular care. If using a slow cooker, boil dry beans on the stovetop for 10 minutes first, then drain and rinse before slow cooking.

  3. Cook the day’s ration.

    Weigh 300 g dry grain and 140 g dry legume. Beans may be easier and safer to cook separately, then combine. Water amounts and cooking times vary, so follow package instructions, add water as needed, and cook until fully tender.

  4. Season after cooking.

    Stir in 2 tablespoons cooking oil and about 1/2 teaspoon iodized salt. People with sodium restrictions should adapt this amount.

  5. Add the boosters.

    Remove from heat. Add the label-verified amount of fortified nutritional yeast, 2 teaspoons moringa powder, and 1 tablespoon seeds. Mix about 200 mg vitamin C powder into water rather than heating it.

Raw sprout safety

Warm, humid sprouting conditions can grow harmful bacteria, even at home. Children, older adults, pregnant people, and people with weakened immune systems should avoid raw or lightly cooked sprouts. Thoroughly cook them or omit them.

Nutrition summary

What this plan can and cannot promise

  • Calories: The menu is intended to approach 2,000 calories, but brands, measurements, cooking loss, and individual needs change the result.
  • Protein: Grains and legumes provide complementary amino acids, but the total amount and digestibility vary.
  • Vitamin B12: Coverage is only assured when the nutritional-yeast label confirms that the daily amount provides at least 2.4 mcg. Unfortified yeast does not count.
  • Vitamin C: About 200 mg is above the adult RDA of 75 mg for women and 90 mg for men, but it is not an individualized recommendation.
  • Remaining gaps: Vitamin A, calcium, iron, essential fats, and other nutrients may be inadequate. Moringa and seed nutrition varies by product.

If you have other emergency foods, add variety. Canned vegetables, fruit, shelf-stable calcium-fortified foods, nuts, and familiar protein sources can make the plan more practical.
